Growth tiering hypothesis

A working cut of the Enterprise book by how much room a state has left, combining outside signals (population and migration, FY27 fiscal outlook, ADA Title II deadlines, the end of ARPA money) with what the book itself shows (ARR per account, capabilities per account, how many accounts sit at three capabilities or fewer). This is a hypothesis to argue with, not a finding.

Tier 1 — Expand

TX, FL, GA, NC, SC, TN, AZ

Population and migration still running ahead of the country, a deep bench of 50,000-plus jurisdictions facing the April 2027 ADA deadline, and shallow footprints in our own book. Net-new logos and first-capability land are realistic here.

Tier 2 — Defend and convert

CA, NY, IL, OH, WA, VA, MD, PA, NJ, MA, CT, CO, MN, MO, MI, WI, DC

Where the money already is, and where FY27 deficits are worst (CA, WA, MD, CO, IL, NY, PA, MN). Growth comes from cross-sell into existing estates — AI, accessibility, records — not from new logos. Renewal risk is the thing to manage.

Tier 3 — Steady

Mountain, Plains and small-budget states: ID, MT, WY, ND, SD, NE, KS, IA, UT, NV, NM, OK, AR, LA, MS, AL, KY, WV, IN, ME, NH, VT, RI, AK, HI

Low ARR per account (North Dakota at $57K, Nevada $55K, New Mexico $59K, Kentucky $78K), thin capability counts, small transformation budgets and few grant programs to lean on. Louisiana and New Mexico also carry real fiscal gaps and flat or falling population. These are high-touch, high-count renewal books rather than growth books.

Sources for the outside signals: Census population estimates through July 2025; Pew, NCSL and MultiState FY26–27 state fiscal outlooks; NASCIO 2026 State CIO Top 10 (AI first, budget and cost control third); DOJ interim final rule of April 2026 moving ADA Title II compliance to April 2027 and April 2028; Treasury's December 31, 2026 ARPA spending deadline.

The states each AE is assigned, the states they hold accounts in, and how many XPs they coordinate with. How this was modeled

  • Segmentation: State and Local ENT accounts are Enterprise, plus DC. Non-SAM special districts are Local SMG; SAM special districts stay Enterprise.
  • Account counts exclude Allocated child records (they carry $0 and travel with the parent). ARR is the export's converted ARR.
  • Enterprise and Local SMG are separate books. No Enterprise XP carries a Local SMG account; each Local SMG AE group moves whole to a dedicated Local SMG XP, so the AE works with one XP instead of several.
  • Jake Sager, Nathan Williamson, Savannah Lane, Kristen Murphy and Ashley Hill carry no accounts in the proposed book. Ashley's current Enterprise accounts route only to her reports; Carolina Prieto is a Team Lead, not a manager.
  • When those three books were removed, each displaced Local SMG AE group moved intact to an XP who already works with that AE. This strictly reduces coordination fan-out without creating a new relationship. The exception is Connecticut PURA: Ashley's reporting-line rule requires one additional Stephanie DelSignore relationship for Steffany while Halena retains the broader Connecticut estate.
  • Carolina Prieto keeps the Kentucky and North Dakota statewide enterprise agreements (two customers; sister agencies are allocated children), plus CHFS, Homeland Security and Transportation for Kentucky consolidation. She releases scattered local accounts to XPs who already work with those AEs. Glendale AZ is the exception: Conrad Taylor has no other book, so it goes to Colleen with her other Pacific SAM work.
  • Local SMG includes special districts except SAM territories. GLAVCD stays Enterprise with Colleen. Luke Mulvaney's three districts sit with Carlos Torres so Halena is not paired with that vertical.
  • AE territories are held in tiering/ae_territories.csv, one row per AE, SAM or vertical seat. Federal and vertical seats have no state territory, and where the source maps do not list states for a territory the row says so; those AEs show only the states they hold accounts in.
