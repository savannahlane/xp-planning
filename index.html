Growth tiering and book balance

Every proposed book is shown by growth tier, ARR and complex-account count so the team can review workload as well as raw account caps. The tiers combine outside signals (population and migration, FY27 fiscal outlook, ADA Title II deadlines, the end of ARPA money) with what the book itself shows (ARR and capability whitespace). Geography is preserved rather than mixing territories solely to equalize tier counts.

Tier 1 — Expand

TX, FL, GA, NC, SC, TN, AZ

Population and migration still running ahead of the country, a deep bench of 50,000-plus jurisdictions facing the April 2027 ADA deadline, and shallow footprints in our own book. Net-new logos and first-capability land are realistic here.

Tier 2 — Defend and convert

CA, NY, IL, OH, WA, VA, MD, PA, NJ, MA, CT, CO, MN, MO, MI, WI, DC

Where the money already is, and where FY27 deficits are worst (CA, WA, MD, CO, IL, NY, PA, MN). Growth comes from cross-sell into existing estates — AI, accessibility, records — not from new logos. Renewal risk is the thing to manage.

Tier 3 — Steady

Mountain, Plains and small-budget states: ID, MT, WY, ND, SD, NE, KS, IA, UT, NV, NM, OK, AR, LA, MS, AL, KY, WV, IN, ME, NH, VT, RI, AK, HI

Low ARR per account (North Dakota at $57K, Nevada $55K, New Mexico $59K, Kentucky $78K), thin capability counts, small transformation budgets and few grant programs to lean on. Louisiana and New Mexico also carry real fiscal gaps and flat or falling population. These are high-touch, high-count renewal books rather than growth books.

Sources for the outside signals: Census population estimates through July 2025; Pew, NCSL and MultiState FY26–27 state fiscal outlooks; NASCIO 2026 State CIO Top 10 (AI first, budget and cost control third); DOJ interim final rule of April 2026 moving ADA Title II compliance to April 2027 and April 2028; Treasury's December 31, 2026 ARPA spending deadline.
