Book check

Cap is 17 countable accounts. Time-zone fit is the XP's zone plus or minus one hour. Complex means more than 7 capabilities; no book may be more than a third complex.

Growth tiers by XP

Each account is scored on growth potential — 60% its territory, 40% the capability whitespace left inside it — and crossed against ARR. Growth load weights the countable book 1.0 / 0.8 / 0.5 / 0.3 by tier. The 17-account cap balances workload; it does not balance opportunity.

Territory tiers

States are scored twice, because the book runs two different motions. State-agency growth turns on the general fund, a named modernization appropriation, and the budget cycle. How this was modeled

  • Segmentation: every State and Local ENT account is Enterprise, with no special-entity overrides, plus DC. That adds 15 accounts the WIP sent to Mid-Market and 4 state agencies new in the 9/21 export: 277 records, 209 countable. The WIP view shows only accounts that were in the WIP.
  • The revised book is hand-built from whole AE territories and checked against an optimizer run. Carolina Prieto keeps her current book except Connecticut and Maine (to Halena), outside the cap and time-zone rule. The open XP2 is a Pacific hire and takes the California state agencies.
  • Capabilities come from Account Product Categories, excluding Service Cloud, Engagement Cloud, Operations Cloud and credits. Only 6 countable accounts have more than 7. Service Cloud Advanced can't be read because Account Products is cut off at 255 characters.
  • Dallas-area accounts (DART, NCTCOG) map to the open Dallas SAM seat. Sarasota Clerk is modeled as North FL with Tampa Bay.
  • Mid-Market (Local SMG) books are left out because neither map covers that segment.
  • Account counts exclude Allocated child records (they carry $0 and travel with the parent). ARR is the export's converted ARR.
  • SAM metros override the base territory: Los Angeles and Glendale CA to Jeff Gaisford, Glendale AZ to Conrad Taylor, San Antonio to Cedric Simpkins, Chicago-area and Columbus-area accounts to Kent Hartsfield (temp SAM).
  • Florida Local ENT is split North (Desmond Davis) and South (Bill Marshall). The map doesn't draw the line, so Tampa and Orlando accounts are modeled as North. Oakland is modeled under Jason Spanier's Other CA rather than the LA/SF SAM.
  • State East/West director comes from which half of the State map the state sits in. Local ENT director is inferred from box shading (pink Andy Barron, green Marty Sargent, blue Lisa Swanson).
  • TX, IA, ME and MI are black on the State map with no AE box pointing to them. Kentucky Tourism (Private Sector) is treated as a Kentucky state agency.
  • Growth tiers cross an account's growth potential (60% territory score, 40% remaining capability whitespace) against its ARR, splitting both at the median of the 209 countable accounts. The two axes are independent in practice: the correlation between log ARR and growth potential is −0.01.
  • Territory scores come from four sourced research reports — state fiscal capacity and IT modernization, the grant and ARPA-cliff landscape, population and permit demand, and Granicus's competitive and purchasing-vehicle position — plus a hand-curated overlay in tiering/state_overlay.csv where every judgement call carries a note. Re-run with python3 tiering/build_tiering.py && python3 tiering/inject_html.py.
  • The biggest caveat is whitespace. "Held / 100k+" counts only this book, so the 83 of 959 jurisdictions it reports is an upper bound on opportunity — Granicus company-wide already serves 48 of the 50 largest US cities. Local-side scores rank states against each other reliably and are inflated in absolute terms until the other segments' books are loaded.
  • Capability data is missing for 16 of the 209 countable accounts, all State-map. Those are imputed at the median depth of State accounts that do have data and marked "capabilities unknown" in the account lists, rather than being scored as pure whitespace.
